Circle Area & Circumference

Understanding why the area of a circle involves r² — and not just r — is one of the most satisfying moments in early geometry. It turns out the area grows much faster than the circumference when you increase the radius.

The Formula

Area and Circumference

$$A = \pi r^2$$ $$C = 2\pi r$$

where $r$ is the radius and $\pi \approx 3.14159265\dots$

Symbol Definitions

Conditions & Assumptions

These formulas apply when:
  • The shape is a perfect circle (all points equidistant from the center) in a flat (Euclidean) plane.
  • The radius is a non-negative real number (r ≥ 0). A radius of 0 gives a degenerate circle (a point) with A = 0 and C = 0.
  • Area units are the square of the length units: if r is in centimeters, A is in cm².

These formulas do not apply to ellipses, spheres, or circles drawn on curved surfaces (e.g., on a globe).

Plain-Language Meaning

The area of a circle is the amount of flat surface it covers. Because it involves r², area grows quadratically with the radius — doubling the radius quadruples the area. The circumference grows linearly: doubling the radius doubles the circumference.

Intuition: Think of the circle as made of thin concentric rings, each with circumference 2πr. Integrating from r=0 to r=R gives ∫₀ᴿ 2πr dr = πR², which is the area formula — a beautiful connection between circumference and area.

Worked Example

Problem

A circular pizza has a radius of 15 cm. Find its area and circumference.

1
Identify the formula and known values.
A = πr², C = 2πr, with r = 15 cm.
2
Calculate the area.
A = π × 15² = π × 225 ≈ 3.14159 × 225 ≈ 706.86 cm²
3
Calculate the circumference.
C = 2 × π × 15 = 30π ≈ 94.25 cm
4
Check units.
Area has units cm² (square centimeters). Circumference has units cm (centimeters).
✓ Area ≈ 706.86 cm² · Circumference ≈ 94.25 cm

Code Examples

Both examples compute area and circumference, check for invalid input, and display results with two decimal places.

#include <stdio.h>
#include <math.h>   /* for M_PI on POSIX; or define pi manually */

int main(void) {
    /* Using a precise value of pi */
    const double pi = 3.141592653589793;
    double radius = 5.0;

    if (radius < 0.0) {
        printf("Error: radius must be non-negative.\n");
        return 1;
    }

    double area          = pi * radius * radius;
    double circumference = 2.0 * pi * radius;
    double diameter      = 2.0 * radius;

    printf("Radius:        %.2f\n", radius);
    printf("Diameter:      %.2f\n", diameter);
    printf("Circumference: %.2f\n", circumference);
    printf("Area:          %.2f\n", area);

    return 0;
}

/* Sample output (radius = 5.0):
   Radius:        5.00
   Diameter:      10.00
   Circumference: 31.42
   Area:          78.54
*/
import math

radius = 5.0

if radius < 0:
    raise ValueError("Radius must be non-negative")

area          = math.pi * radius ** 2
circumference = 2 * math.pi * radius
diameter      = 2 * radius

print(f"Radius:        {radius:.2f}")
print(f"Diameter:      {diameter:.2f}")
print(f"Circumference: {circumference:.2f}")
print(f"Area:          {area:.2f}")

# Sample output (radius = 5.0):
# Radius:        5.00
# Diameter:      10.00
# Circumference: 31.42
# Area:          78.54
Notes:
  • The C example uses a hand-defined π for portability. You can also use M_PI from <math.h> on POSIX systems (not guaranteed by the C standard).
  • Python's math.pi provides the closest double-precision approximation to π.
  • Floating-point arithmetic introduces tiny rounding errors; results like 78.53981… are expected and correct.

Practice Questions

Question 1

A circle has a radius of 4 cm. What is its area to 2 decimal places? (π ≈ 3.14159)

cm²
Hint: A = πr² = π × 4² = 16π. Compute 16 × 3.14159.
A = π × 4² = 16π ≈ 50.27 cm². Remember: area uses r², so a radius of 4 gives area = 16π, not 4π.
Question 2

If A circle has area 28.27 cm², what is its radius to the nearest whole number? (π ≈ 3.14159)

Hint: Rearrange A = πr² to r = √(A/π). Then compute √(28.27 / 3.14159).
r = √(A/π) = √(28.27/3.14159) = √9.0 ≈ 3 cm. Rearranging the area formula gives us the inverse: square-root of A/π.
Question 3

If you double the radius of a circle, the area becomes how many times larger?

Hint: Compare π(2r)² with πr². What's the ratio?
π(2r)² = π × 4r² = 4πr². The new area is 4× the original. This is the key insight: area scales as the square of the radius, so doubling r multiplies area by 2² = 4.
