Question 1 · Geometry

A circle has a radius of 3 cm. What is its area to 2 decimal places?
(Use π ≈ 3.14159)

cm²
Hint: Use the formula A = πr². Substitute r = 3 and compute π × 3² = π × 9.
A = πr² = π × 3² = π × 9 ≈ 28.27 cm². The area grows as the square of the radius — doubling the radius would multiply the area by 4.
Question 2 · Geometry

A right triangle has legs of length 3 and 4. What is the length of the hypotenuse?

Hint: Use c = √(a² + b²). Compute √(3² + 4²) = √(9 + 16) = √25.
c = √(a² + b²) = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5. The 3-4-5 right triangle is one of the most useful Pythagorean triples.
Question 3 · Algebra

For the quadratic equation x² + 2x + 5 = 0, the discriminant b²−4ac = ?
What does this tell you about the roots?

Hint: Discriminant Δ = b² − 4ac. Here a=1, b=2, c=5. Compute 2² − 4(1)(5).
Δ = b² − 4ac = 4 − 20 = −16. Because Δ < 0, there are no real roots. The roots are complex: x = (−2 ± √(−16)) / 2 = −1 ± 2i.
Question 4 · Number Theory

What is gcd(18, 24)? Use the Euclidean algorithm.

Hint: gcd(24, 18) → gcd(18, 24 mod 18 = 6) → gcd(6, 18 mod 6 = 0) → answer is 6.
Step 1: gcd(24, 18) = gcd(18, 24 mod 18) = gcd(18, 6). Step 2: gcd(18, 6) = gcd(6, 0) = 6. So gcd(18, 24) = 6.
Question 5 · Probability

A fair coin is flipped twice. What is the probability of getting exactly two heads?

Hint: The sample space is {HH, HT, TH, TT}. Count outcomes with exactly 2 heads.
There are 4 equally likely outcomes: HH, HT, TH, TT. Only one gives two heads (HH). So P(HH) = 1/4 = 0.25. Alternatively: P(H) × P(H) = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 by independence.
Question 6 · Calculus

If f(x) = x², what is the slope of the tangent line at x = 4?

Hint: Find f'(x) using the power rule: f'(x) = 2x. Then evaluate at x = 4.
For f(x) = x², the derivative is f'(x) = 2x (by the power rule). At x = 4, f'(4) = 2 × 4 = 8. The tangent line at (4, 16) has slope 8.
Question 7 · Counting & Probability

How many ways can you choose a committee of 2 people from a group of 5 candidates? Compute C(5, 2).

Hint: Use C(n, r) = n! / (r!(n-r)!). Here C(5, 2) = (5 × 4) / (2 × 1).
C(5, 2) = 5! / (2! × 3!) = (5 × 4) / 2 = 10. Order does not matter, so each pair is counted exactly once.
Question 8 · Foundations

Evaluate the expression: log₂(64).

Hint: log_b(x) = y asks: "What power must 2 be raised to in order to produce 64?" (2^y = 64).
Since 2⁶ = 64 (2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 64), by the inverse definition of logarithms, log₂(64) = 6.
Question 9 · Foundations

A product's price increases from $50 to $80. What is the percentage change?

Hint: Percentage change = ((x_new − x_old) / x_old) × 100%. Here x_new = 80 and x_old = 50.
Δ% = ((80 − 50) / 50) × 100% = (30 / 50) × 100% = 0.60 × 100% = +60%. Notice that the baseline denominator is the old price ($50).
Question 10 · Statistics

What is the standard deviation of the constant dataset: {7, 7, 7, 7}?

Hint: Standard deviation measures dispersion around the mean. If every item equals the mean, how much deviation is there?
The mean is 7. Each deviation (x_i − μ) = (7 − 7) = 0. Therefore, the variance and standard deviation are both 0. Zero spread means zero variance.
Question 11 · Trigonometry

On the unit circle, what is the exact value of sin(30°) or sin(π/6)?

Hint: In a 30°-60°-90° special triangle, the side opposite the 30° angle is half the hypotenuse.
In a 30°-60°-90° right triangle on the unit circle (hypotenuse = 1), the opposite vertical side (y = sin θ) is exactly 1/2 = 0.5.
Question 12 · Computer Science

What is the asymptotic time complexity of Merge Sort, governed by the recurrence T(n) = 2T(n/2) + O(n)?

Hint: The recurrence tree has log₂(n) levels, and each level does a total of O(n) merge work.
By the Master Theorem (Case 2 with a = 2, b = 2, d = 1), or by summing O(n) work across log₂(n) recursive levels, the total running time is O(n log n).
Question 13 · Linear Algebra & Vectors

Calculate the dot product u · v for vectors u = (2, 3) and v = (4, −1).

Hint: Multiply corresponding coordinates and sum them: u · v = (u₁ × v₁) + (u₂ × v₂).
u · v = (2)(4) + (3)(−1) = 8 − 3 = 5. A positive dot product also shows the acute angle between the two vectors.
Question 14 · Linear Algebra & Matrices

Find the determinant of the 2×2 matrix M = [[3, 2], [1, 4]].

Hint: For matrix [[a, b], [c, d]], the determinant is ad − bc.
det(M) = (3)(4) − (2)(1) = 12 − 2 = 10. Geometrically, this represents the signed area scaling factor of the linear transformation.
Question 15 · Linear Algebra & Vectors

For what value of scalar k are the vectors u = (k, 3) and v = (6, −2) orthogonal (perpendicular)?

Hint: Two vectors are orthogonal if and only if their dot product u · v equals 0.
u · v = 6k + 3(−2) = 6k − 6 = 0. Solving 6k = 6 gives k = 1.
Question 16 · Linear Algebra & Matrices

Under which necessary and sufficient condition is an n×n square matrix A invertible (non-singular)?

Hint: Matrix inverse formula requires dividing by the determinant: A⁻¹ = (1/det(A)) adj(A).
A square matrix is invertible if and only if its determinant is non-zero (det(A) ≠ 0). This is equivalent to having full rank n and linearly independent columns.
