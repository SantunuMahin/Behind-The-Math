Question 1 · Geometry

A circle has a radius of 3 cm. What is its area to 2 decimal places?
(Use π ≈ 3.14159)

cm²
Hint: Use the formula A = πr². Substitute r = 3 and compute π × 3² = π × 9.
A = πr² = π × 3² = π × 9 ≈ 28.27 cm². The area grows as the square of the radius — doubling the radius would multiply the area by 4.
Question 2 · Geometry

A right triangle has legs of length 3 and 4. What is the length of the hypotenuse?

Hint: Use c = √(a² + b²). Compute √(3² + 4²) = √(9 + 16) = √25.
c = √(a² + b²) = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5. The 3-4-5 right triangle is one of the most useful Pythagorean triples.
Question 3 · Algebra

For the quadratic equation x² + 2x + 5 = 0, the discriminant b²−4ac = ?
What does this tell you about the roots?

Hint: Discriminant Δ = b² − 4ac. Here a=1, b=2, c=5. Compute 2² − 4(1)(5).
Δ = b² − 4ac = 4 − 20 = −16. Because Δ < 0, there are no real roots. The roots are complex: x = (−2 ± √(−16)) / 2 = −1 ± 2i.
Question 4 · Number Theory

What is gcd(18, 24)? Use the Euclidean algorithm.

Hint: gcd(24, 18) → gcd(18, 24 mod 18 = 6) → gcd(6, 18 mod 6 = 0) → answer is 6.
Step 1: gcd(24, 18) = gcd(18, 24 mod 18) = gcd(18, 6). Step 2: gcd(18, 6) = gcd(6, 0) = 6. So gcd(18, 24) = 6.
Question 5 · Probability

A fair coin is flipped twice. What is the probability of getting exactly two heads?

Hint: The sample space is {HH, HT, TH, TT}. Count outcomes with exactly 2 heads.
There are 4 equally likely outcomes: HH, HT, TH, TT. Only one gives two heads (HH). So P(HH) = 1/4 = 0.25. Alternatively: P(H) × P(H) = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 by independence.
Question 6 · Calculus

If f(x) = x², what is the slope of the tangent line at x = 4?

Hint: Find f'(x) using the power rule: f'(x) = 2x. Then evaluate at x = 4.
For f(x) = x², the derivative is f'(x) = 2x (by the power rule). At x = 4, f'(4) = 2 × 4 = 8. The tangent line at (4, 16) has slope 8.
Question 7 · Counting & Probability

How many ways can you choose a committee of 2 people from a group of 5 candidates? Compute C(5, 2).

Hint: Use C(n, r) = n! / (r!(n-r)!). Here C(5, 2) = (5 × 4) / (2 × 1).
C(5, 2) = 5! / (2! × 3!) = (5 × 4) / 2 = 10. Order does not matter, so each pair is counted exactly once.
Question 8 · Foundations

Evaluate the expression: log₂(64).

Hint: log_b(x) = y asks: "What power must 2 be raised to in order to produce 64?" (2^y = 64).
Since 2⁶ = 64 (2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 64), by the inverse definition of logarithms, log₂(64) = 6.
Question 9 · Foundations

A product's price increases from $50 to $80. What is the percentage change?

Hint: Percentage change = ((x_new − x_old) / x_old) × 100%. Here x_new = 80 and x_old = 50.
Δ% = ((80 − 50) / 50) × 100% = (30 / 50) × 100% = 0.60 × 100% = +60%. Notice that the baseline denominator is the old price ($50).
Question 10 · Statistics

What is the standard deviation of the constant dataset: {7, 7, 7, 7}?

Hint: Standard deviation measures dispersion around the mean. If every item equals the mean, how much deviation is there?
The mean is 7. Each deviation (x_i − μ) = (7 − 7) = 0. Therefore, the variance and standard deviation are both 0. Zero spread means zero variance.
Question 11 · Trigonometry

On the unit circle, what is the exact value of sin(30°) or sin(π/6)?

Hint: In a 30°-60°-90° special triangle, the side opposite the 30° angle is half the hypotenuse.
In a 30°-60°-90° right triangle on the unit circle (hypotenuse = 1), the opposite vertical side (y = sin θ) is exactly 1/2 = 0.5.
Question 12 · Computer Science

What is the asymptotic time complexity of Merge Sort, governed by the recurrence T(n) = 2T(n/2) + O(n)?

Hint: The recurrence tree has log₂(n) levels, and each level does a total of O(n) merge work.
By the Master Theorem (Case 2 with a = 2, b = 2, d = 1), or by summing O(n) work across log₂(n) recursive levels, the total running time is O(n log n).
Question 13 · Linear Algebra & Vectors

Calculate the dot product u · v for vectors u = (2, 3) and v = (4, −1).

Hint: Multiply corresponding coordinates and sum them: u · v = (u₁ × v₁) + (u₂ × v₂).
u · v = (2)(4) + (3)(−1) = 8 − 3 = 5. A positive dot product also shows the acute angle between the two vectors.
Question 14 · Linear Algebra & Matrices

Find the determinant of the 2×2 matrix M = [[3, 2], [1, 4]].

Hint: For matrix [[a, b], [c, d]], the determinant is ad − bc.
det(M) = (3)(4) − (2)(1) = 12 − 2 = 10. Geometrically, this represents the signed area scaling factor of the linear transformation.
Question 15 · Linear Algebra & Vectors

For what value of scalar k are the vectors u = (k, 3) and v = (6, −2) orthogonal (perpendicular)?

Hint: Two vectors are orthogonal if and only if their dot product u · v equals 0.
u · v = 6k + 3(−2) = 6k − 6 = 0. Solving 6k = 6 gives k = 1.
Question 16 · Linear Algebra & Matrices

Under which necessary and sufficient condition is an n×n square matrix A invertible (non-singular)?

Hint: Matrix inverse formula requires dividing by the determinant: A⁻¹ = (1/det(A)) adj(A).
A square matrix is invertible if and only if its determinant is non-zero (det(A) ≠ 0). This is equivalent to having full rank n and linearly independent columns.
Question 17 · Computer Science & Boolean Logic

According to De Morgan's Laws, what is the boolean negation of the product (A · B)?

Hint: Negating a conjunction switches AND (·) to OR (+) while inverting each term.
De Morgan's first law states: ¬(A ∧ B) ≡ ¬A ∨ ¬B (in circuit notation: (A · B)' = A' + B').
Question 18 · Computer Science & Graph Theory

An undirected graph has 6 vertices, each with degree 3 (a 3-regular graph). How many edges (|E|) does this graph contain?

Hint: By the Handshaking Lemma, the sum of all degrees equals twice the number of edges: Σ deg(v) = 2|E|.
Sum of degrees = 6 × 3 = 18. By Handshaking Lemma: 2|E| = 18 ⟹ |E| = 9.
Question 19 · Machine Learning & Neural Networks

A neural network outputs raw logits z = [2.4, 1.1, -0.5]. After applying the Softmax activation function σ(z), what is the sum of the resulting probabilities across all 3 classes?

Hint: Softmax normalizes exponents by dividing each exp(z_i) by the sum of all exp(z_j).
By definition, σ(z)_i = e^(z_i) / Σ e^(z_j). Summing over all classes gives (Σ e^(z_i)) / (Σ e^(z_j)) = 1.0, producing a valid probability distribution.
Question 20 · Coordinate Geometry & Foundations

Find the coordinates of point P that divides the segment joining A(1, 2) and B(7, 8) internally in the ratio 1 : 2.

Hint: Use the section formula: x = (m·x₂ + n·x₁) / (m + n) and y = (m·y₂ + n·y₁) / (m + n) with m=1, n=2.
x = (1·7 + 2·1) / (1 + 2) = 9/3 = 3; y = (1·8 + 2·2) / (1 + 2) = 12/3 = 4. Therefore, P = (3, 4).
Question 21 · Financial Foundations & Exponents

An investor deposits $1,000 into an account earning 10% annual interest compounded annually ($n = 1$). Using the compound interest formula $A = P(1 + r/n)^{nt}$, what is the total balance after $t = 2$ years?

Hint: Substitute into $A = 1000(1 + 0.10)^2 = 1000 \times (1.10)^2$. Notice $(1.10)^2 = 1.21$.
$A = 1000(1 + 0.1)^2 = 1000 \times 1.21 = \$1,210$. The extra $10 beyond simple interest ($200) represents interest earned upon year 1's interest (exponential compounding).
Question 22 · Algebra & Logarithms

Solve for $x$ in the equation: $\log_3(x) + \log_3(x - 1) = \log_3(12)$.

Hint: By the logarithm product property, $\log_3(x) + \log_3(x-1) = \log_3(x(x-1))$. Set $x(x-1) = 12$ and discard extraneous negative roots.
$\log_3(x(x-1)) = \log_3(12) \implies x^2 - x - 12 = 0 \implies (x - 4)(x + 3) = 0$. Since the domain of $\log_3(x)$ requires $x > 0$, we discard $x = -3$. The valid solution is $x = 4$.
Question 23 · Geometry & Triangle Area

A triangle has sides $a = 13$, $b = 14$, and $c = 15$. Using Heron's formula $A = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$, calculate its exact area.

Hint: First find the semi-perimeter $s = (13 + 14 + 15)/2 = 21$. Then calculate $\sqrt{21 \times (21-13) \times (21-14) \times (21-15)}$.
$s = 42/2 = 21$. Differences are $s-a = 8$, $s-b = 7$, $s-c = 6$. Then $\text{Area} = \sqrt{21 \times 8 \times 7 \times 6} = \sqrt{(7 \times 3) \times 8 \times 7 \times (2 \times 3)} = \sqrt{7^2 \times 3^2 \times 16} = 7 \times 3 \times 4 = 84$.
Question 24 · Geometry & Dimensional Scaling

The radius of a sphere is doubled from $r$ to $2r$. By what factor does the volume of the sphere increase? ($V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$)

Hint: Volume depends on the cube of the radius ($r^3$). Compute $(2r)^3 / r^3$.
$V(2r) = \frac{4}{3}\pi (2r)^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi (8r^3) = 8 \times \left(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\right) = 8V$. While surface area scales quadratically ($2^2 = 4\times$), volume scales cubically ($2^3 = 8\times$).
Question 25 · Trigonometry & Unit Circle

An angle $\theta$ lies in Quadrant II with $\sin\theta = 0.6$. What is the exact value of $\cos\theta$?

Hint: Apply the Pythagorean identity $\cos^2\theta = 1 - \sin^2\theta$. Remember in Quadrant II, $x = \cos\theta < 0$.
$\cos^2\theta = 1 - (0.6)^2 = 1 - 0.36 = 0.64$. Taking the square root gives $|\cos\theta| = 0.8$. In Quadrant II, cosine is negative, so $\cos\theta = -0.8$.
Question 26 · Counting & Probability

In poker, how many distinct 5-card hands can be dealt from a standard 52-card deck without replacement? Evaluate $C(52, 5)$.

Hint: Order does not matter in a hand of cards, so divide the permutation $P(52, 5) = 52 \times 51 \times 50 \times 49 \times 48$ by $5! = 120$.
$C(52, 5) = \frac{52 \times 51 \times 50 \times 49 \times 48}{5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = \frac{311,875,200}{120} = 2,598,960$ distinct combinations.
Question 27 · Bayes' Theorem & Base Rate Fallacy

A medical condition affects 1 in 1,000 individuals ($P(D) = 0.001$). A diagnostic test has a 99% true positive rate ($P(T|D) = 0.99$) and a 1% false positive rate ($P(T|D^c) = 0.01$). If a patient tests positive, what is the approximate posterior probability $P(D|T)$ that they actually have the condition?

Hint: Out of 100,000 people, 100 have the condition (99 test positive) and 99,900 do not (~999 false positives). Calculate $99 / (99 + 999)$.
By Bayes' Theorem: $P(D|T) = \frac{P(T|D)P(D)}{P(T|D)P(D) + P(T|D^c)P(D^c)} = \frac{0.99 \times 0.001}{0.00099 + (0.01 \times 0.999)} = \frac{0.00099}{0.01098} \approx 9.01\%$. The extreme rarity of the condition causes false positives to outnumber true positives by roughly 10 to 1.
Question 28 · Calculus & Product Rule

Differentiate the function $f(x) = x^3 \sin(x)$ with respect to $x$.

Hint: Product Rule states $(uv)' = u'v + uv'$. Let $u = x^3$ and $v = \sin(x)$. Recall $\frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x)] = \cos(x)$ and $\frac{d}{dx}[x^3] = 3x^2$.
$u = x^3 \implies u' = 3x^2$; $v = \sin(x) \implies v' = \cos(x)$. Applying $(uv)' = u'v + uv'$ yields $3x^2\sin(x) + x^3\cos(x)$.
Question 29 · Linear Algebra & Matrix Transformations

A 2D linear transformation is defined by matrix $M = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$. If a shape in the plane has an initial area of 1 unit², what is its area after transformation?

Hint: The area scaling factor of a 2D linear map is given by the determinant: $\det(M) = ad - bc$.
$\det(M) = (6)(4) - (2)(2) = 24 - 4 = 20$. In linear algebra, the determinant measures how much areas are stretched or squished by the matrix operator. The transformed area is $1 \times 20 = 20\text{ units}^2$.
Question 30 · Computer Science & Search Complexity

You perform a Binary Search on a sorted array of 1,000 items. In the worst case, what is the maximum number of comparisons required to locate an element or conclude it does not exist?

Hint: Binary search cuts the problem in half each step: worst-case comparisons = $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor + 1$. Note $2^9 = 512$ and $2^{10} = 1024$.
Because $2^9 = 512 < 1000 \le 1024 = 2^{10}$, the maximum number of iterations is $\lfloor\log_2(1000)\rfloor + 1 = 9 + 1 = 10$. Each comparison eliminates half of the remaining interval.
